Introduction & PurposePlateful is a connected system of three applications built around a single dining experience: a customer discovering and ordering food, a restaurant running its day-to-day operations, and a waiter or captain serving tables on the floor. The three applications share information instantly — an order placed by a customer or a captain appears on the restaurant's console within seconds, and every table's status stays in sync across all three screens.This document lists every feature planned for the platform, grouped into the three applications, and within each application split into Core Features (the essential functionality needed for day-one operation) and Enhanced Features (additions that improve the experience and can be added in the same build or shortly after).No technical or engineering detail is included here by design, so that this document can be reviewed and discussed purely on what the system does for the people using it — customers, owners, managers, and staff.2. Platform Strategy: Web Application & Native Mobile ApplicationPhase 1 — Web Application (Starting Point)All three applications — Customer App, Manager Console, and Captain App — will initially be delivered as a single, modern web application. This means nothing needs to be downloaded or installed: a customer scans the table's QR code and the menu opens immediately in their phone's browser; restaurant staff log in through any tablet, computer, or phone browser to reach their respective screens.Despite not being a downloadable app in this phase, the experience can still be “added to the home screen” on a phone, appearing with its own icon and able to send notifications — giving much of the feel of a real app without the cost or delay of publishing one. This approach keeps the initial cost low and allows the system to be live and usable quickly, which matters most while the idea is still being proven with a real restaurant.Phase 2 — Native Mobile Application (Future Option)Once the business has grown and the demand for a dedicated app is clear, the same system can be extended into proper downloadable applications listed on the Apple App Store and Google Play Store. This gives the restaurant its own branded app icon on customers' phones, smoother day-to-day performance, and deeper access to phone features such as richer notifications and camera tools.This step is entirely optional and does not require rebuilding the system from scratch — the work done in Phase 1 carries forward, with only the on-screen experience being rebuilt as a native app on top of the same foundation.3. Customer ApplicationThe customer-facing app is how diners discover the restaurant, view its menu, order, book a table, and leave feedback after a genuine visit.3.1 Core FeaturesQR Code Menu Viewing — When a customer scans the QR code placed on their table, the full menu opens instantly on their phone — every dish shown with a real photograph, its price, and a plain-language description. Nobody has to guess what a fancy dish name actually means or what will arrive at the table.Group / Shared Table Ordering — The first person to scan a table's QR code becomes that table's order host. If friends at the same table also want to order from their own phones, they send a join request that the host can accept or decline — keeping one organised order per table instead of several conflicting ones.Table Pre-Booking with Visual Seating — Customers can reserve a table in advance. After choosing the number of guests, they see a visual layout of the restaurant's actual seating — much like choosing a seat for a movie. Tables that do not match the group size are automatically locked, so a two-person booking cannot occupy an eight-person table.Time-Verified Reviews — Once a customer's bill is marked as paid, a short review window opens immediately, allowing them to rate the food, presentation, and ambience separately. The window closes quickly afterwards, ensuring every review comes from a genuine, recent, paying visitor — not an anonymous or unrelated rating.Customer Account & History — Each customer has a personal account that keeps track of past orders, restaurants previously visited, saved preferences such as vegetarian or non-vegetarian choices, and a wishlist of favourite places to revisit.Restaurant Photo & Story Feed — Customers can browse photographs and short-lived “stories” posted by the restaurant — showing dishes, the dining space, and special occasions — helping them know what to expect before they arrive.Search & Discovery — Customers can search for and explore details about the restaurant directly within the app, including its menu, photos, and ratings.3.2 Enhanced FeaturesLoyalty Rewards — Returning customers earn points or small rewards for repeat visits, giving them a reason to come back rather than try somewhere new.Refer-a-Friend — Customers can invite friends to the app. When a friend visits for the first time, both the customer and their friend receive a discount.Birthday & Anniversary Offers — Using the date a customer adds to their profile, the app can automatically send them a small offer around their birthday or anniversary.Direct Feedback Channel — Customers get a private way to send feedback or raise a concern directly with the restaurant owner, kept separate from public reviews.Reorder — Customers can quickly repeat a past order in one tap, without browsing the full menu again.Favourites — Customers can save specific dishes they love for faster access the next time they visit.Multiple Languages — Key screens and the menu can be viewed in more than one language, for example Hindi and English.Split Billing — A group can split the final bill evenly between everyone, or by what each person actually ordered, instead of one person paying it all.Digital Waitlist — When the restaurant is full, customers can join a waiting list from their phone instead of standing near the entrance.Special Occasion Requests — While booking a table, customers can request small arrangements for occasions such as birthdays or anniversaries.4. Restaurant Manager ConsoleThe Manager Console is the restaurant's operational command centre, used by the Owner, Managers, and Reception staff on a tablet or computer at the restaurant.4.1 Core FeaturesSales & Performance Dashboard — A central screen showing today's sales, monthly and yearly trends, the best-selling dishes, and an overall sense of customer satisfaction — giving the owner a clear picture of the business at a glance.Table Management & Live Billing — A visual layout of the restaurant's floor showing every table's live status — available, occupied, or awaiting payment. Staff can open a table, view its order, and generate the bill without leaving this screen.Online Order Management — Incoming orders from delivery platforms or the restaurant's own website arrive here in real time, with simple accept or decline actions, and accepted orders are routed straight to the kitchen.Billing History & Reports — A searchable record of past bills — by date, table, customer, or payment type — with the ability to reprint a receipt or review how a bill was settled.Staff & Captain Management — Owners and managers can create login credentials for waiters and captains, and organise which tables or sections each staff member is responsible for.Inventory & Expense Tracking — A simple way to log day-to-day costs — ingredients, supplies, repairs — and keep track of raw-material stock levels.Menu Management — Owners and managers can add, edit, price, and remove dishes, along with uploading the photograph that customers see on the QR menu.Social Media & Promotions Management — From this same console, the restaurant can post photos, short-lived stories, today's specials, and time-limited offers that appear directly inside the customer app.Role-Based Access (Owner / Manager / Reception) — The Owner sees everything, including financial figures and staff management. Managers and Reception staff see only what they need for daily operations, with sensitive financial views hidden unless the Owner permits it.4.2 Enhanced FeaturesStaff Performance Insights — A simple view showing how many bills each staff member closed and how quickly, helping recognise strong performers.Low-Stock Alerts — Instead of only logging stock after the fact, the system proactively warns the owner before an ingredient runs out.Shift Handover Notes — A short note one staff member can leave for the next, so nothing important gets missed at a shift change.Customer Feedback Inbox — Direct messages or complaints sent by customers land in one place for the owner to review and respond to.Automated Sales Reports — A daily or weekly summary of sales is generated automatically, ready to be shared by email or WhatsApp.Discounts & Coupons — The owner can create time-limited discounts, percentage-off offers, or happy-hour pricing without involving anyone technical.Table Merge / Split — For large groups, two or more tables can be combined into a single bill, or one table's order split across several bills.Multi-Counter Billing Support — Larger restaurants can run more than one billing counter, each handling its own set of tables.Staff Attendance Log — A simple record of when staff log in and out for their shifts.Exportable Reports — Sales and billing data can be downloaded as a PDF or Excel file for record-keeping or accounting.5. Captain / Waiter ApplicationThe Captain App is a lightweight, mobile-first tool used by waiters and captains while moving between tables, designed for speed during a busy service.5.1 Core FeaturesSecure Staff Login — Each captain logs in with their own credentials, issued by the Owner or Manager, so every action can be traced to the right staff member.Assigned Table Overview — After logging in, the captain sees only the tables they are responsible for, along with each table's current status.Order Taking & Kitchen Sync — Captains can take a customer's order directly on their phone or tablet; the order appears instantly on the Manager Console and at the kitchen printer.Add Items & Settle Bill — Captains can add more items to an open table's order at any time, then move the table to settlement once the customers are ready to pay.Payment Confirmation — For cash payments, the captain simply marks the bill as settled. For online payments, the captain takes a quick photo of the successful payment screen as proof before the table is freed up for the next guests.5.2 Enhanced FeaturesManager Alert (SOS Button) — A single button a captain can press to immediately notify the manager about a complaint, an issue at a table, or anything needing urgent attention.Regular Customer Notes — Captains can note a returning customer's known preferences, so service feels personal even on a busy night.End-of-Shift Summary — A short summary at the end of a shift showing how many tables the captain served and the total amount billed.Works Without Internet — Orders taken during a brief internet outage are queued on the device and sent automatically once the connection returns, so service never has to stop.Voice-Assisted Order Entry — Captains can speak an order instead of typing it, speeding up order-taking during a busy rush.6. A Note on Future GrowthEverything described in this document is scoped for a single restaurant. The platform is, however, being thought through in a way that does not block future growth — if this proves successful, the same system can later be extended to serve multiple restaurants on one shared platform, each with its own branded experience, without needing to start over.End of Document
